# Rainkeep

A complete desert water-survival strategy game for phones, in 3D. The rain stopped a generation ago. You raise the last Rainwyrm, a water dragon whose cooling mist keeps your keep alive. You dig wells so your people have water to drink and the wyrm has water to breathe, read the horizon for sandstorms and heatwaves, shelter survivors, recruit heroes, send caravans across the Dunes and ride with your Caravan. The story runs in three acts: six chapters to the Sunheart, the fallen shard of sun that boiled the sky dry; four more through the floods and salt marshes of The Long Rains to the Ember Throne; and The Wyrmsong, five chapters past the Burning Line to free the last wyrm's sealed kin and wake the Mother of Rains.

- **Play:** open `index.html` in any browser, or install it to your phone's home screen (see below).

## What's in the game

| Area | Contents |
|---|---|
| **3D world** | The keep and the Dunes are real-time 3D scenes (three.js, vendored): low-poly desert architecture that changes with building level, a day and night cycle, sandstorms, dust haze, heatwaves and rain showers, villagers walking to work, camels, palms swaying in the wind, an animated oasis and the Rainwyrm itself. The keep is a terraced oasis at the head of a canyon: the spring in a sunken stepped basin, houses and barracks on raised terraces, an upper town with the Rain Altar, the watchtower on its own crag, stone stairs between the levels, cliff dwellings and the Temple of Rains carved into the canyon walls, and a walled front gate. The buildings follow their paintings: plastered and mudbrick walls, carved lattice windows that glow at night, turquoise-glazed and zellige-tiled domes with gold finials, the Deep Well's domed pavilion and water wheel, and roofs full of life (jars, rugs, sunshades, potted bougainvillea, washing). The streets have a zellige ring around the spring, a market of spice stalls with their sellers, string lanterns and bunting over the avenue, brass lanterns on every post, flowers and airing rugs on the walls, and handcarts. The people are painted 3D models made with Higgsfield from full-body paintings: ten kinds of sand dweller (an elder in a keffiyeh, a Tuareg veiled in indigo, a Bedouin woman in an embroidered thobe, a spice trader in a fez, a farmer with a basket on his back, a grandmother, a water carrier, a village woman and two children) walk the lanes and mind the market stalls; every Steward stands by the building they look after and the squad waits in the plaza, pacing now and then (tap one for their sheet); the six companions trot, sit and fly about the keep. The camels on the caravan road, the Scorpion raiders who march on the gate and the beasts of the Dunes (jackal packs, sand lions, oryx, ironback tortoises and circling vultures) are painted models too. Until the models arrive (or where they cannot load), drawn villagers and animals stand in. The camera keeps one fixed angle: drag to move around, pinch or scroll to zoom (toward your finger, from the whole canyon down to street level), double-tap or press the compass to fly home. Falls back to a 2D renderer on devices without WebGL, and can be switched off in Settings |
| **The Rainwyrm** | A serpentine water dragon in 3D: a sculpted head with golden slit eyes, a hinged jaw and fangs, ear frills and long whiskers, a scalloped fin sail down its back, fan fins and a tail fan, belly plates and iridescent scales. 20 levels, 9 forms that grow its fins and frills and lengthen and branch its horns, a rain cloud of its own, a crown of storm horns (Stormcrowned) and finally a halo and river of living water (Skyriver), 3 mist settings and Attuned mist (the wyrm sets its own from Lv 6), **Call the Rain** (from Lv 3: a shower that fills the wells, cools the keep and calms sandstorms, on a recharge, with Rain Charms for emergencies), petting and naming, an Ascension choice at Lv 12 (Monsoon, Mistveil or Floodheart), Wyrm's Torrent that opens every battle, **Cloud Run** (from Lv 5: the 3D wyrm itself flies over the dunes three times a day; steer it through rain clouds and golden drops, thread rain rings for a combo that makes every cloud worth more, catch Rain Pearls, dodge dust devils, and bring the clouds home as water, Starglass and bond; pearls buy six **Wyrm Gifts**: more hearts, longer flights, a cloud magnet, more golden drops, more water, and Storm Rider), 6 skins |
| **Survival** | Water is life: survivors drink it and the wyrm breathes it as cooling mist. If the wells run dry, the wyrm sleeps, the heat pours in and families leave. Forecast weather (dust haze, sandstorms, heatwaves) gets hotter as the wyrm grows, middays are hotter and desert nights cold; heat bands drive sickness and productivity; food, housing, raider attacks with a Storehouse to protect stock, offline protection |
| **The keep** | 13 buildings on fixed plots across three levels (Deep Well, Date Grove, Sandstone Quarry, Copper Mine, Mudbrick Houses, Healer's House, Barracks, Watchtower, Archive of Rains, Caravan Hall, Storehouse, Sunsteel Forge), worker assignment (auto or manual), 12 research lines with 15 levels each, 3 troop types in a counter triangle |
| **Building Charters** | When a kind of building first reaches Lv 10 it takes one of two charters for good, chosen on its sheet: a Deep Well's Artesian Bore (water +8%) or Covered Cisterns (the keep drinks 10% less), a Quarry's Deep Cut or Masons' Guild (building and research 6% faster), a Date Grove's Irrigated Rows or Shade Canopies, a Copper Mine's Rich Seam or Bellows Works, the Houses' Cool Courtyards or Night Watch, the Healer's House's Herbalists or Field Surgeons, the Barracks' Drill Yard or Quartermaster, the Watchtower's Far Sight or Signal Fires, the Archive's Old Records or Rain Charts, the Caravan Hall's Hostelry or Duelling Ring, the Storehouse's Sealed Vaults or Tally Clerks, and the Forge's Blast Furnace or Armorers. The first charter is free; changing one costs 100 Starglass. |
| **Warden's Gear** | The Sunsteel Forge (from Rainwyrm Lv 12) smelts copper into Sunsteel. Six pieces of gear (blade, shield, cloak and one for each troop class) climb 50 levels through five tiers and strengthen every squad you send out |
| **Keep life** | Surplus bubbles to tap over working buildings, 16 keep incidents (travellers at the gate, a fever, a buried cistern, a wedding under the palms) with choices that cost something and pay off in boosts, survivors or setbacks, and travelling merchant caravans that camp by the gate and swap what you have too much of for what you're short on. **Channels**, a water puzzle: turn stone channel pieces until the spring reaches every hut, palm and field (puzzles open four at a time per Rainwyrm level, three stars against par, star chests, a daily puzzle, dowsing hints; tap the irrigation channel or the side button). **The Rainwyrm's bond**: the wyrm makes small wishes tied to normal play (rain, dates, a Downpour, a story from the Dunes), and granting them raises a 10-level bond with lasting perks. **Hold the Gate**: sighted raiders march across the dunes to the gate; rain when they arrive weakens them, and boiling water from the walls steadies your defenders. **Keep gardens** (from Rainwyrm Lv 6): nine monuments with their own place in the keep, from a fountain inside the gate and twin wyrm statues before the temple to a Sunsteel sculpture and a glass mosaic court, each raised over five levels for a small lasting bonus and modelled in the 3D keep as it grows |
| **The Dry Season** | From Rainwyrm Lv 6, a drought every three days of keep time that lasts twelve hours, sighted two hours ahead: the wells give 25% less, the keep drinks 15% more and it is 3°C hotter, and the spring sinks in the 3D keep. The Warden answers each with one edict: **Ration the Water** (a third less drinking, production -10%), **Dig a Deep Cistern** (stone and copper; the wells hold up, and every cistern adds 1% to water for good, up to 10%) or **Keep the Rain Watch** (Call the Rain comes back twice as fast and lasts longer, but the Torrent is 20% weaker). When it ends the keep is graded A to C on thirst and sickness, and the Warden's chest pays by the grade. |
| **Warden's Decrees** | From Rainwyrm Lv 5, orders for the whole keep, pinned on the side rail. Six open one by one with the Rainwyrm: **Harvest Rite** (production +30% for 2 hours), **Rush Order** (every build, research and training under way finishes a fifth sooner, up to an hour each), **Call to Arms** (+12% attack for an hour), **Open Roads** (marches that reach a node gather 40% faster for 2 hours), **Feast of Rain** (cures the sick, healing +50% for 4 hours) and **Wyrm's Vigil** (the Torrent +30% for an hour). Each rests 8 to 24 hours after it runs its course, so the choice is when; a resting decree can be given again early for Starglass (30 an hour of rest left). A chip on the keep screen counts down the decrees in force. |
| **Companions** | From Rainwyrm Lv 7 desert animals come to live in the keep: Sahra the fennec arrives first, then Layl the sand cat, Hudhud the hoopoe, Rimaya the Arabian oryx, Saqr the saker falcon and Nimr the caracal, each tamed once the keep has grown enough. They grow to Lv 30 on **Honeyed Dates**, with an Advance at Lv 10 and 20 that takes **Camel Bells**; every level raises a keep bonus (food, copper or water production, building and research speed, squad attack or troop strength) and each has a skill on a cooldown: a cache of food and water, copper and Starglass, an Urgent Message that hurries every timer, or an hour of stronger gathering, attack or troops. Treats forage by themselves and come from beasts on the Dunes and the duty chests; bells from every expedition boss, every tenth Spire floor, the Crossing's hidden oasis, the last duty chest and now and then a strong beast. Painted portraits and 3D models made from them; the tamed animals roam the 3D keep (tap one to open it) |
| **Rival Keeps** | From Rainwyrm Lv 8, eight other keeps stand on the Dunes, each with a warden, a standing and a storehouse, drawn as painted 3D forts flying their own colours. Scout one (a little food) to see its defenders and stores, then march your squad on it: a win carries home a third of its storehouse plus Starglass and journals, and the beaten keep raises a Peace Shield for 90 minutes while its stores fill back up. A keep you raid may answer with its warband, which arrives as the next raid on your gate, sighted from the watchtower like any other; beat it or not, strike back within the hour for +20% attack. Your own Peace Shield (150 Starglass, 2 hours) keeps every warband away, and drops the moment you attack someone. Standings rank you against the eight by strength; keeps out in the dust haze come into reach as the Rainwyrm grows |
| **Pacts & Feuds** | Every rival keep holds you in some regard, from -100 (a feud) to 100, shown on its sheet. A gift (once every 6 hours a keep) raises it by 20, a raid lowers it by 35, and it drifts back toward 0 by 4 a day. At 50 a keep will sign a pact (one at Rainwyrm Lv 8, two at 12, three at 16): it sends tribute every 8 hours, its warriors stand on your walls when raiders come (+10% defense a pact), and your marches won't ride against it. Breaking a pact turns the keep into an enemy at -100. A keep in a feud sends its warband against your gate on its own every 6 to 10 hours, so the choice is which keeps to befriend and which to raid. |
| **Far Journeys** | From Rainwyrm Lv 5, a board of five journeys beyond the Dunes (a new board every 8 hours, or early for 60 Starglass), each sending a party of up to three heroes away for 2 to 8 hours. A journey of one to four stars has one to three requirements the party must meet together (a class, stars between them, an Epic or Legendary hero, a hero's level, a full party of three), each drawn so the keep's own heroes can meet it; a Best party button finds the cheapest party, sparing the squad. Heroes away can't fight. Rewards grow with the stars (journals, food, treats, whetstones, Starglass, Road Dice, a Beacon Token, speedups), and every hero in the party comes home with 1 to 4 shards of their own. Two parties at a time at first, four by Rainwyrm Lv 15. |
| **Oasis Outposts** | From Rainwyrm Lv 7, claim a resource node on the Dunes as an outpost: builders march out with a garrison (at least a tenth of your march cap), and when they arrive a palisade goes up and the garrison stays. The outpost yields the node's resource every hour (more for higher nodes, half again on a flooded oasis, Sunsteel on a vein) and keeps eight hours of it for you to collect, and its walls rise to Lv 5. Every 4 to 7 hours a raider band comes for it, as many fighters as a tenth to a fifth of your march cap, fresh recruits at your Barracks level, so what tells is the garrison you leave, its ranks, your gear and the walls (+8% defense a level). You get ten minutes' warning to send reinforcements; a garrison that loses is routed, half of it limps home and the outpost falls. One outpost at first, up to four by Rainwyrm Lv 19. Garrisons count as troops out for housing, ranks and the Hall of Wardens. On the 3D Dunes each outpost is a ring of stakes with tents, a lookout from Lv 3 and your banner. |
| **Trade Routes** | From Rainwyrm Lv 10, trade caravans leave the keep for four markets beyond the Dunes: Saltmarch Bazaar (2 hours there and back), the Southern Wells (3), the Copper Coast (4) and the Glass Cities (6). Each posts two orders at a time, new ones every 6 hours of keep time: goods it wants (scaled to the keep) for things the keep can't make (journals, Desert Whetstones, Sunsteel Caches, speedups, companion treats and bells, Road Dice, copper, Starglass, an Epic Shard Pouch). Each market's prices move from day to day (×0.8 to ×1.4). The far roads pay best and cross worse bandit country (15% to 40% risk); hired guards (none, half or full, paid in copper and food) cut the risk, and a caravan caught on the road loses half its payment. Two caravans at once, three from Rainwyrm Lv 15. Camel trains walk off the edge of the 3D Dunes towards their market and back. |
| **Gate Defenses** | From Rainwyrm Lv 5, three works at the front gate, raised with resources up to Lv 10: **Ballista Towers** (your defenders hit raiders harder), **Oil Cauldrons** (they hold better) and a **Stake Yard** (raiders arrive weaker). They help against every raid and rival warband, and at Lv 5 and Lv 10 each brings one more of its tactic (Ballista bolts, Fire Pots, Stakes) to every Scorpion Siege. They stand on the walls in 3D: more ballistas on the towers, more cauldrons on the wall top and more rows of stakes on the sand as they rise |
| **Troop Ranks** | From Barracks Lv 10 the drill sergeants raise troops through three ranks: **Veterans** (+10%), then **Elites** (+20%, Barracks Lv 14) and **Champions** (+32%, Barracks Lv 18). A drill takes the Barracks the way training does (speedups work on it), moves a batch of one class up one rank and costs that class's own resources, scaled to the keep. A class fights at the average strength of all its troops, so every drill lifts the whole class, and in a fight the recruits fall first: ranks only thin out once a class has lost all its recruits. Troops out marching keep their ranks. A banner for each open rank stands before the Barracks and flies once any troops hold it. |
| **Wadi Clash** | From Rainwyrm Lv 9, a live three-way battle for a dry canyon's water rights, played in its own canvas overlay. You and two rival caravans each have three squads (yours are your squad heroes, each leading a third of your march, ranks and gear included). Seven points stand in the wadi: three towers, two wells, the Rain Shrine and the Old Cistern, each scoring its value every second its holder keeps it. Tap a squad, then a point. Squads that meet fight at once by Lanchester's law (the stronger side wins and keeps the square root of the difference of the squares), the losers limp home to heal, and a point stays yours after your squad leaves until someone takes it. The rivals' captains go after whoever is leading. First to 700, or the leader after three minutes, wins; rewards by place (Starglass, Desert Whetstones, journals, Oasis Wars points). A Clash Banner comes every 4 hours (three at most); leave mid-match and your captains finish it, or hand them the squads with Auto. The **Clash League** ranks every match: a win earns 40 league points, second 10 and third loses 20, never dropping you below your tier, across six tiers (Sand, Copper, Silver, Gold, Sunsteel, Rainwyrm). Each tier up brings rivals 5% stronger and match rewards 15% richer. Seasons last a week of keep time: the highest tier reached pays Starglass, whetstones and shards by mail, and the next season starts two tiers lower. |
| **The Camel Derby** | From Rainwyrm Lv 8, a racing camel, Saffron, and live races on the salt pan below the keep, drawn on a 2D canvas over the painted course. Train her Speed, Stamina and Spirit (Lv 1 to 20, one session at a time, for food and keep time) and race her against five rival camels in three cups (the Village Cup, the Oasis Stakes and the Desert Crown), each opening when you win the one before and bringing a piece of tack (Sand Pads, Braided Bridle, Racing Saddle) the first time. Hold to urge her: an urged camel runs faster and keeps her pace over the dunes, but burns energy, and one that runs dry slows for a few seconds; the rivals ride as sprinters, closers or steady riders. A race entry every 4 hours (three at most); leave mid-race and the jockey rides it out, or hand him the reins with Auto. |
| **Spring Fishing** | Since the rains came back the keep's spring has fish in it (from Rainwyrm Lv 3). Tap to cast, tap again the moment the float dips (too soon and the fish scatter, too late and it's gone), then hold to reel and let go when the fish runs: reeling against a run piles on tension, and too much snaps the line. Six kinds, from Spring Minnows (food) and Golden Barbs (Starglass) to Glass Eels (journals), Old Whiskers the catfish (Desert Whetstones) and the Rain Koi that only rises after rain (Starglass and a Beacon), each with a size, a first-catch double reward and a page in the catch book. A cast comes back every hour (six at most), and calling the rain brings two more |
| **The Cookfire** | From Rainwyrm Lv 4, every fish landed at the spring also goes into the larder, and the cookfire turns fish and food into dishes that serve the whole keep for a few hours, two on the table at once and never the same one twice: Minnow Skewers (gathering +20%), Sand Carp Stew (production +8%), Golden Barb Pilaf (squads 6% harder), Glass Eel Broth (half the sick cured, healing +50%), a Whiskers Roast (troops 3% stronger) and the Rain Koi Banquet (production +6%, squads 8% harder, the Torrent +10%). |
| **The Buried City** | From Rainwyrm Lv 11, a city under the sand dug out layer by layer. Each layer is a 6x7 grid hiding five relics (two of two tiles, one of three, one of four and a grand relic of six, either way round). A Trowel digs one tile, and dug sand shows how many relic pieces lie in the eight tiles around it, so a careful digger clears a layer in about 24 Trowels and a careless one in about 40. Bedrock (from layer 4) takes two Trowels; a Blasting Charge clears a 3x3 patch. Relics pay by size (copper, whetstones, speedups and journals, Starglass), 4% more per layer down; clearing a layer opens its chest (Starglass and Trowels, a Charge every third layer, shard pouches at layers 5, 10, 15, 20 and every 10 after). Trowels come free every 45 minutes (up to 12), from beasts and bosses, and from the $1.99 daily Digger's Kit. |
| **Relic Charms** | Every kind of relic dug up in the Buried City can be worn by one hero as a charm, chosen on the hero's sheet: the small relics give 1% to 1.5% a level (health, defense, attack or skill power), the three-tile ones 1.2% to 1.5%, the four-tile ones 1.5% to 2%, and the grand relics 2% to 2.5%. A charm's level is the number of its kind found, up to 10, so every layer dug keeps the charms growing. One charm per hero and one hero per charm. |
| **Field Orders** | From stage 20, Captain Hadi posts three orders a day, each a stage you've cleared (a few to thirty behind the front) to fight again under a condition: win with a given Breath Art, with a given formation lead, without the breath, with only your two strongest heroes, within four rounds, or ending with 70% of the squad's health. The art, lead, breath and heroes are set for the fight; the result has to be earned (live play reaches it early). Each order pays 20 Starglass and Field Journals, all three a Beacon Token; a missed order can be fought again. The Orders sheet is in the Rewards hub |
| **Heroic Chapters** | Win all thirty stars in a chapter and its Heroic version opens under the chapter's stars on the Expedition tab: the same ten stages, fought in order, each foe as strong as a stage fifteen further on and carrying two traits (a boss three), so the squad, formation and Breath Art all have to fit. Each Heroic chapter features one hero (Bashir, Amira, Kofi, Yara, Rashid, Samira, Tomas, Sefa and Noor in the first nine, then the Legendaries Leila, Tariq, Zahra, Nadia, Soraya and Idris): a first clear pays 20 Starglass (40 on a boss) and 3 of that hero's shards (6), bringing the hero to the keep if they aren't there yet, and a won stage can be raided at once for 2 shards (3 on a boss) and Field Journals, three raids a day across every chapter. The Heroic sheet in the Play hub lists every open chapter with its hero and a raid |
| **Breath Arts** | The Rainwyrm breathes once a fight, and in expedition battles, star replays and the Leviathan's hunts the breath can be more than a Torrent (everywhere else it stays a Torrent). From Rainwyrm Lv 6 it can breathe a **Mist Veil** (heals the squad, softens the next two blows and draws out venom) and from Lv 11 a **Riptide** (40% of a Torrent's damage, but the foe is held under for two rounds: no blows, no regeneration, and a frenzy calmed). Every art breaks a wind-up. Each answers different foe traits (the Torrent cracks a sand-shell, the Veil answers venom, the Riptide regeneration), so the choice is made against the foe, one tap on the stage card, the star replay sheet or the Leviathan's sheet; a trait no hero answers offers the art that does. On a plain foe all three need about the same strength; the Veil and Riptide are what take the Unbroken star |
| **Stage Stars** | Every story stage holds three stars: the victory, **Unbroken** (ending the fight with half the squad's health or more) and **Swift** (winning within five rounds). A first clear at the edge of the squad's strength rarely takes all three, so any cleared stage can be fought again from the Expedition tab's stage list (browse back through the chapters) for the stars it is missing; a replay pays only stars and has no limit. Each chapter's thirty stars fill three chests at 10, 20 and 30 (journals and supplies, then Starglass and Desert Whetstones, then a Beacon Token), and the Stars sheet in the Rewards hub lists every chapter's chests. The live battle is where the last stars are won: the breath into a wind-up, a Mend before the squad drops below half |
| **Foe traits** | From expedition stage 16 every foe has a trait (a boss two), each with an answer: **Armored** (half again the defense; Sunder cuts twice as deep), **Regenerating** (heals 5% a round unless a skill strikes it that round), **Venomous** (4% of the squad's health a round; Mend cures it and wards for two rounds), **Frenzied** (10% harder every round; the Torrent calms it) and **Sand-shelled** (half damage for three rounds; the Torrent cracks it). A foe whose trait a hero's skill answers is 4% weaker otherwise, so a squad that brings the answer comes out ahead. The stage card shows each trait and whether the squad can answer it; the battle shows them under the foe's name and in the log; the auto-battle plays the answers. |
| **Ready now** | One list of everything waiting for the player (idle builders, an idle Archive or Barracks, a quest to claim, and every feature that marks itself ready: attacks left, Trowels stacked up, a decree ready, dishes to cook), each with a button that opens it and a way back; a standing Ready entry on the rail shows the count. |
| **The Founding Week** | A new keep's first seven days (opens at Rainwyrm Lv 2, for a keep no further than Lv 6): five missions open each day (upgrades, expedition stages, recruits, troops, the Dunes, the Rainwyrm's level), 35 in all at 20 points each, collected one by one or all at once. Six chests stand along the 700-point track (journals and speedups, Starglass and Beacon Tokens, an Epic Shard Pouch) and the last is a Legendary Shard Pouch: any Legendary hero the player chooses. A day opens each real day since the week began or after each 24 hours of keep time; the week stays open two days past the seventh, then earned chests come by mail. A $4.99 Founding Chest is on sale once during the week. |
| **Hero Kinships** | Twelve pairs of heroes whose stories are tied (the Glasshands, the Wyrm-Singers, the Keep Kitchen, Shield and Shade...). A kinship forms once both are recruited and works whenever both are in the squad: one bonus for the whole squad (attack, defense, health, the Torrent or damage taken), 5% to 9% at Lv 1 with the humbler pairs getting more, rising by a quarter at each level (4, 6, 8 and 10 stars between the two). No hero is in two kinships, so a squad has at most one at work. Shown on the roster, on each hero's sheet and on its own sheet. |
| **The Sparring Ring** | From Rainwyrm Lv 10, up to four heroes (six with 300 and 600 Starglass) sit in the ring on the Heroes tab and fight at the level of the third-best hero outside the ring, never past their own level cap, so stars still matter. They keep that level in the squad, on a march or anywhere else they fight. A seat whose hero leaves rests for 12 hours. It makes kinship partners, heroes whose skill answers a foe's trait and far-journey parties worth fielding without levelling every hero. |
| **The Sand Leviathan** | From Rainwyrm Lv 9, a leviathan too big to kill surfaces in the deep dunes for a hunt of one day of keep time. Three attacks a hunt, each a full 12-round battle (live, with the breath and hero skills) scored by the damage done before the squad falls back or the rounds run out; it fights as one troop class each hunt, so the squad that counters it does most. The best attack ranks you among the Hall of Wardens' fifty (their attacks are their share of your par by power, with luck on the day); par is what the strongest squad to attack this hunt does on auto-battle (it only ever rises, so a weak first attack can't set it low), and four marks for beating it (60% to 150% of par) pay at once, so playing the fight well pays as well as strength. The rank pays by mail when the Leviathan dives. From the second hunt it also carries a foe trait (Armored, Regenerating, Venomous, Frenzied or Sand-shelled), so the squad that answers it does more. A daily duty, three achievements, Oasis Wars points. |
| **Formations** | Choose which troop class leads your marches and expedition battles: Balanced, or Shieldbearers, Archers or Lancers making up most of the march. The class that counters a foe hits it 20% harder, but the classes fight differently (Shieldbearers are tough and hit softly, Archers hit hard and break easily), so wherever a fight is offered the formation picker fights it out in advance with each formation and marks the one that fares best |
| **Heirlooms** | Every hero carries one thing from before the keep: Zahra's lantern from the Glass Cities, Bashir's thirty-year well rope, Omar's emergency pick that nobody has ever seen him use, Yusra's grandmother's conch. It wakes when the hero reaches 3 stars and is tempered with **Desert Whetstones** up to Lv 10; each level adds 2.5% to the hero's attack, defense and health and 4% to their skill. Whetstones come from watchtower errands and bounties, the siege and Crossing chests, the Bazaar on the Spice Road, now and then a beast, and the daily Heirloom Kit |
| **Hero Talents** | From Rainwyrm Lv 6, a hero chooses one of two talents at levels 10, 30, 50, 70 and 90, named for their class (a Shieldbearer's Iron Hide or Deep Lungs, Shield Captain or Rallying Cry, and so on). Each tier sets the hero's own attack, defense or health against something for the whole fight: the troops of the hero's class +5% or +8% (attack, defense and health), the skill +15%, the skill ready a round sooner, +10% more edge for the hero and their class over the class they beat, or the Rainwyrm's Torrent +10%. The first choice at a tier is free; changing it costs 50 Starglass. A gold dot on the roster marks a talent waiting to be chosen. |
| **Hero Awakening** | From Rainwyrm Lv 12, a hero at 5 stars can be awakened five times with its own shards (3, 5, 7, 9 and 12 duplicates' worth for its rarity), one more step at every second Rainwyrm level (A1 at Lv 12, A5 at Lv 20), so recruits keep counting once a hero is fully starred. Each awakening adds 4% to the hero's attack, defense and health and 10 levels to its cap; A3 strengthens its skill by 10% and A5 by 20%. The card sits under the stars on the hero sheet, and awakened heroes wear an A badge on the roster. |
| **Hall of Wardens** | From Rainwyrm Lv 4, a ranking of fifty wardens of the Dunes by power, you among them (troops out marching still count). The other 49 are made once per save and follow a typical free player's power curve, taken from the balance bot, each at its own share of it (0.3 to 2.2 times) and its own pace, so some race ahead early and are caught later. A free player who plays well sits around rank 15 and a steady spender around rank 6. ## Play it

No build step is needed:

- Open `index.html` in a browser (browsers will not load the 3D models from a page opened as a file, so the keep shows its drawn villagers, animals and no heroes), or
- Serve the folder (`npx serve rainkeep` from the repo root) and open it on your phone over the same Wi-Fi. When served over https, the game also works offline.
- On iPhone, open it in Safari and choose **Share → Add to Home Screen** to play full screen.

Progress saves in the browser. Settings (gear icon) has sound toggles, the 3D graphics switch, save codes and **Start a new keep**. Add `?flat` to the URL to force the 2D renderer.

## Tuning and testing

Change numbers in `data.js` and reload. Timers and production run about 30× faster than a typical live-service strategy game, so the two-act story takes a couple of weeks of evenings and the endgame a good while longer. For quick testing, open the browser console:

```js
rainkeep.advance(600)                            // fast-forward 10 minutes of game time
rainkeep.grant({ stone: 5000, water: 5000, starglass: 2000, beacons: 10, speed60: 3 })
rainkeep.act('fight')                            // any action the buttons use
rainkeep.state()                                 // the full save object
KH.town3d.zoom(1.5); KH.town3d.drag(0, -100)     // move the 3D camera
```

**Balance bot.** `tools/balance-bot.cjs` plays the whole game headless and prints milestones, resource sources, builder idle time and errors. Install Playwright once (`npm i -D playwright && npx playwright install chromium`), then run `npm run balance -- f2p 36` (modes: `f2p`, `founder`, `dolphin` at about $150, `whale` at about $1,150; purchases are simulated). It also prints where Starglass went, by action, and what blocked each next Rainwyrm level. `npm run balance -- f2p 36 5 sgspend` makes it spend Starglass only on recruits. Compare several runs: gacha luck moves results by an hour or more.

The game was balanced with an automated player that plays every system (building, research, troops, heroes, gacha, marches, ruins, Caravan, raids, events, duties, surplus, rain, incidents, merchants, gear, the Spire, Duels) for 36 hours of game time. See DESIGN.md, section 8, for the resulting pacing.
